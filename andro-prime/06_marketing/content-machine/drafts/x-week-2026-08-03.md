## Sunday, thread

**Post by hand.** Metricool will not split X copy into a thread, so this one is manual until that is tested.

**1/**
> Why "your testosterone is normal" might not mean what you think.
>
> A thread on where that range actually comes from.

**2/**
> The NHS range is roughly 8 to 29 nmol/L.
>
> It is not a healthy band. It is a statistical one: the spread you get from sampling adult men aged 18 to 70, then cutting at the 2.5th and 97.5th percentile.

**3/**
> Who is in that sample?
>
> Athletes. Sedentary men. Lean and overweight. 22-year-olds and 68-year-olds.
>
> All in one pot.

**4/**
> Age does most of the work.
>
> Testosterone peaks in the early 20s and falls roughly 1 to 2% a year from 30.
>
> By 45 the average man has lost 15 to 30% of his peak. By 60, more than 40%.
>
> That decline is baked into the range.

**5/**
> The population moved as well.
>
> Body fat contains aromatase, which converts testosterone into estradiol. UK obesity has nearly tripled since 1980.
>
> The range gets resampled over time. Normal today is lower than normal was thirty years ago.

**6/**
> So the range is wide because it was built to fit men decades apart in age and in health.
>
> Not because it describes a well man.

**7/**
> None of this means the framework is broken. It answers the question it was built for.
>
> It is just not the question you are asking when you feel worse than you did five years ago.
>
> Full article: https://andro-prime.com/blog/myth-of-normal-range
