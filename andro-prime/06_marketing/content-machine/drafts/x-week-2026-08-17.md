**Rails, written as what the copy does** (an allowlist, per the CONTEXT.md rule that a note listing prohibited terms re-triggers the scanner on itself):

- Describes thresholds, geography and mechanism. Makes no statement about any individual reader's level.
- Every figure is quoted from the canonical article: the NICE bands (under 25 deficient, 25 to 50 insufficient), 50 to 75 adequate, the 75 to 125 band from the Endocrine Society 2011 guideline, the 125 toxicity-watch line, UK latitudes of 51 to 56 degrees north, the October to March synthesis window, the ~95% sunscreen figure, the ~3 week serum half-life, and the February-versus-August seasonal contrast.
- Names the escalation the article names: under 25 nmol/L is a GP conversation, and the thread says so in its last post rather than burying it.
- Routes nowhere except the article itself. No kit, no quiz, no list, even though Kit 2 does measure this marker. Week 1 set that precedent and it is kept.
- Punctuation stays within the brand rule: colons, commas, semicolons, full stops, brackets.

**The scope of this batch, stated as what it covers.** Thresholds in nmol/L. UK latitude and the October to March synthesis window. Sunscreen and body composition as mechanisms. The serum half-life and the seasonal shape it produces. The symptom picture men report. The GP escalation at the bottom band. **That list is the whole scope, and it is deliberately narrower than the article's.**

**Two of the article's findings sit outside it, by decision.** Both are intervention findings: the article's hormone-association paragraph and its respiratory meta-analysis. **Founder post (Wednesday), scope note.** Line 1 is the LinkedIn headline approved under **CA-029** on 2026-07-28, near-verbatim ("I find what's broken in systems nobody can see into. Banks, then businesses, now men's blood results."). Line 2 is an editorial observation about unread results and carries no health claim. **What this post deliberately does NOT do is claim a Vitamin D history for Keith.** The rotation asks for a founder line and the honest founder line here is about the pattern, not about his own levels, because no such reading is on record. **If Keith wants a personal Vitamin D line in this slot, it has to come from him**, and it would be net-new copy needing its own pre-flight rather than inheriting this article's.

**On the seasonal timing, which is the reason this week works.** Mid-August is the annual peak for UK Vitamin D (the article's honest read is two snapshots: trough in February and March, peak in August and September). A reading taken now says which end of the swing a man starts the dark half of the year from. This is the same angle already cleared and published on Facebook in `put-it-down-to-age`, so the messaging is consistent across channels rather than newly invented here.

**1/**
> You are at your annual Vitamin D peak right now. By February you will be at your trough.
>
> Most UK men have never seen either number.
>
> A thread on why that happens.

**2/**
> Start with geography, because it does most of the work.
>
> London sits at 51 degrees north. Birmingham 52. Newcastle 55. Edinburgh 56.
>
> From October to March the angle of UV-B over Britain is too low for skin to make meaningful Vitamin D.

**3/**
> Then lifestyle stacks on top.
>
> Indoor work through the middle of the day, when it would have counted.
>
> Reasonable sunscreen use drops skin synthesis by around 95%. Not an argument against sunscreen: skin cancer risk wins that trade easily.

**4/**
> Body composition matters as well.
>
> Vitamin D is fat-soluble. It gets stored in fat tissue, and it is less available to you when there is more of that tissue to store it in.

**5/**
> The UK bands, in the units your own lab prints:
>
> Under 25 nmol/L: deficient
> 25 to 50: insufficient
> 50 to 75: adequate for most
> 75 to 125: the band most active men want
> Above 125: toxicity watch

**6/**
> What it feels like is rarely loud.
>
> Tiredness that sleep does not shift. Mood that drops with the daylight. Recovery that takes two days where it used to take one. A dull ache you cannot pin on any particular session. More colds than you used to get.

**7/**
> One reading is a snapshot, not a verdict. Vitamin D is the most seasonal marker on a standard panel.
>
> Under 25 nmol/L is a GP conversation this week, not a supermarket aisle one.
