## Deviations from the queue brief (both flagged to Keith, neither made silently)

**1. The "three things" count was not written.** The queued angle for L-02 was "Your GP tested three things. There were four worth seeing." The first half asserts a specific count of what a standard NHS panel contains. That count is nowhere in the canonical article, it varies by practice and presentation, and the standing rail is real numbers only. The "four worth seeing" half is exact and survives.

**2. The opening was swapped at pre-flight (Keith's call, 2026-07-28).** The first draft opened "A standard blood panel is built to rule out disease. It was never built to explain why you feel like this," and a later paragraph repeated the same idea ("that panel did the job it was designed to do"). Pre-flight raised it as amber: low ASA risk, fair and non-disparaging, but it characterises what a standard panel is designed for, and the canonical article never makes that claim. Under derivative discipline the most prominent line in the post was saying something its source does not.

Keith chose the inside-the-source rewrite over an Ewa referral. Both instances were replaced: the opening became a statement about which question a result answers, and the later paragraph now credits the clear result as good news before making the turn. The post asserts nothing about clinical practice anywhere, which is what took it from amber to green with no fresh Ewa step.

**The general rule this run establishes:** when a derivative's strongest line is the one that exceeds its source, rewriting inside the source is usually better than escalating. It ships the same week, it keeps Ewa's queue for things that genuinely need a clinician, and the constrained version is often the sharper line.
