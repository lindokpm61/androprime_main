> Most men who type "male menopause" into a phone at one in the morning are not looking for a number. They are looking for a word.
>
> Because a word would mean it is a real thing, and that it has a name, and that he is not imagining it.
>
> The NHS calls "male menopause" an unhelpful term. Not because nothing is happening. Testosterone does fall as men age, roughly 1% a year from around the age of 30 to 40, and over a decade that adds up. It is unhelpful because it suggests a single switch-off moment in middle age, and that is not what happens. In many cases the symptoms have nothing to do with hormones at all.
>
> So the word is doing two jobs and only one of them honestly. It gives the feeling a name. It does not tell you the cause.
>
> That second job is the one that costs men years, and every symptom list online has the same hole in it. Low energy, low mood, poor sleep, a softer sex drive. Those can be age-related testosterone change, or they can be sleep, stress, or lifestyle. Same handful of symptoms, several different causes, and no checklist separates them.
>
> Which is why the format works on all of us. You read fourteen items at one in the morning, you recognise yourself in nine, and you close the tab believing you have got the thing the list is named after. That is not you being gullible. It is what the format is built to make you do.
>
> You do not have a symptom problem. You have a which-cause-is-it problem.
>
> A list can only ever hand you a better word. The one thing that separates the causes is measuring, and that gap is why I am building Andro Prime.
>
> If you have ever spent an evening on a symptom list, what did you actually do with it the next morning?
