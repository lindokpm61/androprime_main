POST

> Testosterone isn't the only thing that changes as men get older. The protein that holds on to it changes too, and that one is rarely measured.
>
> It's called SHBG, sex hormone binding globulin. Your liver makes it. It binds tightly to testosterone. It carries it around your blood in a form your body can't use.
>
> Think of it as a set of handcuffs. Cuffed testosterone is still in your blood, and it's still counted in your total. It just can't act on anything.
>
> In men, about 45% of testosterone is bound tightly to SHBG. Most of the rest is loosely held by a protein called albumin. Only around 1 to 3% is completely free (Lab Tests Online UK).
>
> Ageing tends to push SHBG up. So do liver disease, an overactive thyroid, higher oestrogen levels, and some anticonvulsant medicines.
>
> Which puts a man in his late forties somewhere odd. His total has held up fine over the years. It reads mid-range, much as it always has. What's changed is how much of it is cuffed, and the total counts the cuffed and the free as if they were the same thing.
>
> What changed wasn't his testosterone. It was the thing holding it.
>
> The bend runs the other way too. Carrying excess weight and having an underactive thyroid both tend to lower SHBG, which can make a total look lower than the free fraction really is. That's the reason SHBG belongs on the panel at all. Without it, you can't tell which way your total is being bent.
>
> The free androgen index, the figure half the internet tells you to chase, isn't what UK labs report for men. North Bristol NHS Trust reports a calculated free testosterone for men, and reports the free androgen index for women. When researchers compared FAI against calculated free testosterone in men, it explained less than half the variation in the figure it stands in for (Ho et al., 2006).
>
> So if your total is ambiguous and your SHBG is unusual, calculated free testosterone is the figure to ask for. If you already have an FAI sitting on a report, read it as a rough signal rather than as your usable number.
>
> One honest limit, because it narrows everything above. If your SHBG sits inside its reference range, a calculated free testosterone tells you nothing your total hasn't already told you (SfE and ACB joint position statement, 2023). These figures earn their keep when SHBG is high or low, not as a routine upgrade on every test.
>
> And one line that doesn't move. A total testosterone below 12 nmol/L is a GP conversation, not a wellness one. UK guidance regards the borderline 8 to 12 nmol/L band as a reason for further assessment rather than reassurance (BSSM, 2023). We measure and we explain the number, and we tell you when the next step is your GP. We don't diagnose, and we don't interpret your results one by one.
>
> Education, not medical advice.
>
> Ask for SHBG alongside the total. One extra line on the panel, and the total finally reads properly.
>
> The longer version, with the reference ranges and the sources, is here: https://andro-prime.com/blog/free-androgen-index?utm_source=facebook
