## Script (v2, rewritten to voice 1.2 on 2026-07-31)

**Emotion:** Recognition / relief.

**Addiction loop.** Stakes: Mark is flat by two every afternoon, nothing is obviously wrong, and he has a fortnight before he decides to spend money on something. Big question: if nothing has changed, why does he feel worse. Head fake: a blood testing company is about to tell him to buy a blood test, and instead tells him not to yet. Unguessable in advance, obvious in hindsight, and it is the conflict-free positioning said out loud. Rehook: "and if it does move, you have just saved yourself the price of a test."

**Structure:** B, Problem-solver. Devices 6 (banal scene) and 3 (physical object for an invisible state) stacked.

POST linkedin

> A man I know went to his GP because he was tired all the time.
>
> Bloods came back normal. He left with nothing to do.
>
> I asked him what had changed in the six months before that appointment.
>
> He thought about it. Nothing, really. Same job. Same training. Same sleep, roughly.
>
> That "roughly" is where these things live.
>
> He'd been in bed eight hours a night and getting maybe six of broken sleep. A drink most evenings. A weekend lie-in that dragged his body clock two hours in each direction. Training volume up, easy weeks gone. A breakfast that spiked him and dropped him by mid-afternoon.
>
> None of it looks serious written down. That's exactly why it gets missed.
>
> Silence, when I listed it back to him.
>
> He didn't have one big problem. He had five small ones stacked, and no way to see the stack.
>
> So here's the honest sequence. Change two or three of the inputs. Hold them for a fortnight. See whether your energy moves. A single tired week tells you nothing. A fortnight of real changes tells you whether the cause is on that list.
>
> And if it doesn't move? Then a blood test earns its place. Vitamin D. Active B12. Ferritin, your iron stores. Plus hs-CRP, a protein the liver releases when the immune system is irritated. Common, measurable things worth seeing when tiredness won't shift.
>
> It isn't a diagnosis on its own. It's a baseline instead of a hunch, and a better conversation if you take it to your GP.
>
> There's a line here that matters more than the rest. Tiredness that's lasted weeks despite real changes, or that comes with unexplained weight loss, breathlessness, or a low mood that won't lift, is a GP conversation. Not a wellness one. We don't diagnose and we don't prescribe.
>
> Most men reading this will recognise at least two things on that list.
>
> Which two have you been calling "roughly the same"?
