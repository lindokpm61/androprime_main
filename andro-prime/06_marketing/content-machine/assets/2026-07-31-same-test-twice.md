## Chosen hook

Spoken: "I had the same blood test twice, six weeks apart. 7.69, then 10.32. Both came back inside the normal range."
On-screen text (frame 1, muted-legible): "7.69. Then 10.32."
Archetype: Experimenter. Scored 12/12 against `hook-rubric.md`.

## Script

**Emotion:** Surprise / curiosity.

**Addiction loop.** Stakes: Keith wants an answer, and there are six weeks between the only two data points he has. Big question: which of the two numbers is actually him. Head fake: the viewer predicts one of them came back abnormal and that is the story; the reveal is that both were called normal and the gap between them is a third. Rehook: "which would be fine, except the range they were both inside runs from 6 to 22."

**Structure:** A, Personal story.

```
[0-3s]   I had the same blood test twice, six weeks apart.
         [Visual: NHS App test result history on the phone. Thumb scrolls
         once. Both rows land in frame together. Real screen, not a graphic.]
         [Text: 7.69. Then 10.32.]

[3-9s]   First one came back 7.69. Second one, 10.32.
         [Text: Six weeks apart]

[9-16s]  That is a third higher. Same man. Same test.
         [Pause]

[16-27s] Both were reported inside the range. Which would be fine,
         except the range on my sheet runs from 6 to 22.
         [Slower]

[27-40s] That is the bit that got me. A range that wide has room for two
         versions of you, and it will call both of them normal.
         It was never built to tell you how you are doing.
         It was built to spot illness.

[turn]   I am Keith. I started a blood testing company, and that is
         genuinely why. Not because the range is wrong.

         was asking.

[CTA]    When did you last see your actual numbers, instead of the word
         normal?
         [Text: Education, not medical advice]
```

*Four-check: interesting to Mark, because the swing is something he has never considered. Compressed, roughly 50 seconds, nothing unload-bearing. The hook stands alone with no audio. End emotion is surprise, aimed and landed. Compliance, stated as what the copy does: it reports two figures that are Keith's genuine NHS results (7 Oct and 18 Nov 2025); it leaves the change between them unexplained; it describes the width of a printed reference interval; it points the contrarian energy at that interval; it discloses the founder at the turn; it stays inside Kit 1's live marker; it routes to the quiz. Every clinical decision point is absent by design.*

## Notes

**Claim inheritance.** Canonical is `myth-of-normal-range` (published, Ewa-signed). Inherited: the range was built to flag illness rather than define wellness; a wide range calls materially different men the same word. Added: nothing.

**The one place this diverges from the canonical article.** The article states the NHS range as roughly 8 to 29 nmol/L. Keith's reporting lab printed 6 to 22. Reference intervals are assay and lab specific, so both are correct, but the script must say "the range on my sheet" and never "the NHS range" or it contradicts a live article.

**What is deliberately not in this script, and why.** Keith's 7.69 sits below the 8 nmol/L NHS referral line named in the canonical article, and both figures sit below the `< 12` low-testosterone cut in `04_products/results-engine/thresholds.md`. Ewa's ruling D of 2026-07-29 (`03_compliance/correspondence/2026-07-30-keith-ewa-fai-rulings-a-to-d.md`) cleared Keith showing his own results and said the call is his. It did not cover setting those results against a clinical cut-point, because that was not the question asked. Stating the numbers is reporting; naming them as low is interpretation. This script does the first only.

**Open question for Keith, worth resolving before filming.** A viewer who knows the 8 nmol/L line will see 7.69 and wonder why it goes unmentioned. The script is honest without it, and the point it makes is about range width rather than about Keith's health. But the stronger and arguably more transparent version says what 7.69 means, and that version needs a fresh named ruling from Ewa.
