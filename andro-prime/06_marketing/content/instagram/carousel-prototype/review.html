The band crop, now real

found and fixed 2026-08-11

video.js documented that the brand band is cropped off before animating and composited back afterwards, so the lockup never passes through a video model. The code did not do it. The word crop appeared once in the file, inside that comment. Anyone following the file's own documentation spent money and got a clip with a destroyed lockup, while the comment read as reassurance that it was handled. It would have cost all ten clips, not one.

“The band is cropped off before animating and re-composited afterwards: a burnt-in band would be warped by the video model along with everything else, and the lockup has to stay pin sharp.”

The step now exists, and it refuses rather than guesses. The band height is read from work/band.png — the same strip inpaint.js re-attaches, and never a constant, because the band is baked at a different height in every base (195px on base-5 to 263px on base-1). Before cropping anything, the script compares the source's bottom strip against that band and aborts below 0.97 SSIM: a band-free frame scores 0.034 and a different cover geometry is refused on width alone. The blind crop it replaces would have silently eaten 262px of photograph.

Afterwards the clip is scaled to cover the image area and centre-cropped, never stretched, then the band is composited over the bottom of every frame at 1080×1350. Models return their own aspect — wan gave 560×704 from a near-square frame — and stretching to fit widens the face, which is the failure already documented for Higgsfield.

Verified without spending anything: a synthetic clip was pushed through the real code path and the band region of the output measures 0.992 SSIM against the band as composited, the residual being h264 alone. The raw model output is now kept beside the finished clip, because a missing band and a mangled band look identical afterwards — which is exactly how the first reading of the test below blamed the model for a step that was simply absent.

Checking the finished cover against the template found two more gaps of the same shape, both now fixed. The video cover is three layers — animated photo, brand band, then the type layer carrying the eyebrow, headline plate, rule and footer. build.js writes that type layer as cover-overlay.html and its comment said “composited over the mp4 by video.js”. video.js had never heard of it and render.js did not rasterise it, so an animated cover would have shipped as a photograph with no headline on it. And build.js defaulted a missing coverPhoto to the vitamin D cover, so a B12 deck would have put its own title on the plate over a newspaper reading “14 signs of low vitamin D” — the one mismatch the single title table exists to make unrepresentable, reintroduced by a || and invisible to the existence check because the fallback file exists.

Both are closed. video.js takes --deck, refuses a deck whose type layer has not been rendered, and says so loudly when no deck is passed; the default photo is gone. Frame 1 of a composited clip now matches png/<deck>/cover-video.png with the type region at 0.991 SSIM.

So the pair below is not a model difference. The wan clip is raw model output with the band sent through the model, which painted over it with table, mug and laptop. The Kling clip is a finished shortlist asset whose band was composited back afterwards. Kling would have eaten it too.

Decided

Two cover formats, alternating

Keith · 2026-08-11

Keith's finding re-opened a design that had passed every other check. Every call until then was judged on a single carousel. Instagram's profile grid shows slide 1 only, so a first-time visitor sees thirty covers at once, and that is the surface deciding whether they follow. All ten covers sat on one base photograph, so the grid was one image thirty times. The schedule spaces a topic's three appearances ten tiles apart, so no two neighbours match; it made no difference, because what you notice is the photo.

Five reference grids Keith supplied settled it (an AI-tools creator, a health creator, Rethink Testosterone, Chris Williamson, GenAI.works). They share exactly one feature: the headline is a graphic overlay on the image, large and high contrast, never baked into the scene. One of them runs the same headshot in all thirty tiles without reading as a bot, because the logos and a red headline bar carry the variation. The repeated photograph was never the problem. Ours is small, angled, low contrast and printed on newsprint, so the only element that varied was the one you cannot read.

The decision: alternate two cover formats. A video cover (photo + printed newspaper headline + an overlay plate) and a typographic cover, so the same title in both forms looks like two different posts. The newspaper headline stays: newsprint is the scene, the plate is what makes the tile legible at grid size. An earlier version of this page called the newsprint headline a defect and recommended replacing it; that was wrong and is retracted — see the archive.

This does not retire the inpainting. The mask, the ~£0.07 per swap and video.js's pin-the-newspaper prompt work are all still load-bearing. Twenty unique covers, not thirty: ten video, ten type.

The assignment rule is not the obvious one. “Alternate” reads as day % 2, which produces immaculate cell counts and silently locks every topic to one format for the whole run, because 2 divides 10. It is (topic + appearance) % 2: 15 video, 15 type, 10 posts per close, no topic locked. A 2×3 factorial, so both main effects read across the full run. plan.js refuses to print a schedule that fails checkBalance().

Decided

One title table, three consumers

covers.js · 2026-08-11

covers.js holds one row per topic and every cover asset is generated from it: the newspaper headline inpainted into the photo, the overlay plate on the video cover, and the typographic cover. The newsprint and the plate say the same words on purpose. Setting them from one row makes a mismatched cover unrepresentable — and a mismatch is not obviously broken to whoever schedules it, it just looks like a mistake nobody made on purpose.

The line break is stored, never computed. inpaint.js takes two lines and the newsprint layout depends on where the break falls, so it is an editorial decision.

The mask, and one headline swap proven end to end

2026-08-10

The cover has its headline printed into the photograph, so a thirty-post run needs thirty headline swaps. The mask is cut against this exact layout, at x 350-785, y 452-750 on the 1122 × 1140 image area, against measured ink extents rather than by eye. One swap is proven: the same photograph carries “Why am I always tired?” with every region outside the box measuring 1.000 SSIM against the source, above, below, left and right. That is the property the whole run rests on. About £0.07 a swap.

Route matters. Masked inpainting on Replicate (Ideogram v3) keeps every pixel outside the box identical, which is what holds the character steady across a grid. Higgsfield cannot do this at all: no model in its catalogue accepts a mask input, so every edit re-renders the whole frame. Reconstructing the mask afterwards was tried and fails, because the editor re-frames the newspaper and the new headline runs outside the old box and gets sliced mid-word.

The mask is valid only for this cover geometry. Change the base photo and it must be re-cut.

Six base photographs, and the eyes

base-2 selected · 2026-08-10

One fixed base photo, edited per article, rather than a new scene each week. Same man, same kitchen, same breakfast every week — which is what prompt-only generation could never give us. The six live bases are 1122 × 1402, already black and white, already carrying the brand band.

At feed size the subject read as though his eyes were shut. At full resolution none of the six are: all are downcast, with heavy lids and a furrowed brow, which is what compresses into a closed look in a thumbnail. Two independent editors were asked, in two separate wordings each, to lift the lids while keeping the gaze down on the page. All four attempts raised the gaze to camera. The models treat eyelid aperture and gaze direction as coupled and drop the gaze constraint, so the raised gaze is a capability limit rather than a prompt failure, and was accepted as a deliberate change.

Produced with Seedream 5 Pro via Higgsfield, then corrected back to true geometry. Higgsfield returns the requested aspect ratio exactly and stretches to reach it, so the raw 2048 × 2048 render is about 1.6% too wide. The band was cropped off before the edit and composited back afterwards, so the lockup never passed through a model.

The three shortlisted animated covers

2026-08-10 · vitamin D only

Image-to-video from an approved still, fitted to 1080 × 1350 at 24fps, 121 frames, with the brand band composited back on afterwards. These three exist for the vitamin D topic; the other nine are owed. video.js can now produce them correctly — the band crop these three were made with by hand is in the script as of 2026-08-11.

Why these three and not new generations. Higgsfield does not hold the character well enough to re-render the cover per article. Measured against the untouched regions of the source photo, the masked Replicate route scores 0.965 to 0.984 SSIM while the best Higgsfield editor manages 0.933 to 0.965. Small per post, but it compounds across a thirty-post grid, which is the one thing the fixed base photo exists to prevent. Kling 3.0 on Higgsfield also has no negative_prompt, so only low-motion scenes survive.
