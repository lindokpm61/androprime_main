## The live privacy policy names the wrong hosting processor (2026-08-21)

**Verified by fetching `https://andro-prime.com/privacy` and reading the served text**, not the repo. Prompted by Keith saying the policy had been rectified. It has: the page is **v1.2, July 2026**, synced to live at the `BUNDLES_ENABLED` flip (commit `248bcc1`), and it is accurate on almost everything: Art 9(2)(a) explicit consent, the Recheck Bundle's automated-scheduling disclosure, the retention schedule, ICO number ZC172852, company 17185839.

**Note the drift is structurally impossible between repo and live**: `app/(marketing)/privacy/page.tsx` reads `canonical-site/privacy/index.html` at request time, so the served page IS the repo file. What can drift is the file against reality, and that is what has happened.

### The defect

The processor table (source: `privacy/privacy-policy.md:200`, rendered at `canonical-site/privacy/index.html`) reads:

| Vercel | Website hosting | Usage/request data | Processor |

**We do not use Vercel.** `09_website-app/CONTEXT.md:3` names the stack: *"Next.js 15 · Supabase (Ireland) · Stripe · Customer.io · Upstash QStash · **Coolify (VPS)** · **Cloudflare**"*. There is no Vercel dependency in `package.json`. So the policy:

1. **names a processor that is not one**, and
2. **omits two that are** — the Coolify VPS host, and Cloudflare, which this workspace's own `CONTEXT.md` §4b documents as edge-caching our served objects (measured 2026-08-14: assets returning `cf-cache-status: HIT` with the origin already gone). A CDN that caches responses processes request data.

Under UK GDPR **Art 13(1)(e)** the recipients or categories of recipients must be disclosed accurately. Naming the wrong one is a transparency defect, not a typo. It is also the kind of thing a SAR or an ICO query surfaces immediately, because the customer can check.

### Also still open: the 2026-07-25 legal review, item 10

The transfers section still reads, verbatim: *"Some of our service providers are based outside the UK or process data on servers outside the UK. Where this applies, we ensure appropriate safeguards are in place, including UK IDTA-standard contractual clauses or adequacy decisions."*

- **Still generic.** It does not name the mechanism per US processor, which is exactly what the legal review asked for.
- **Customer.io's actual basis is unstated.** The DPIA relies on SCCs + UK Addendum auto-incorporated **plus DPF UK-Extension certification**. Neither "Data Privacy Framework" nor "UK Addendum" appears on the live page (0 occurrences each).
- **"UK IDTA-standard contractual clauses" muddles two alternatives.** The IDTA and the UK Addendum to the EU SCCs are separate instruments; you use one or the other. The hyphenated compound reads as a single named thing that does not exist.
- **Ireland is not stated on the policy** (0 occurrences), while `components/account/DataPrivacySection.tsx:48` tells the logged-in customer *"Your data is stored in the European Union (Ireland)"*. Not a contradiction, since silence is not a conflicting claim, but reconciling the two is the other half of item 10.

### Not a gap, despite appearances

The DPIA's §5 lists *"update privacy policy to describe the low-T nurture purpose + Art 6(1)(a)/9(2)(a) basis"* as Pending, and `nurture` returns 0 hits on the live page. **That is correct sequencing, not an omission:** the low-T nurture opt-in is itself not built (§5: "Pending — gates nurture activation"). A privacy policy should describe processing we actually do. The update is owed **at activation**, not now.
