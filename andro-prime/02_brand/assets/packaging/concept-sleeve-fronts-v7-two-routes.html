What drawing it changed

Correction Route B is not the weaker match

I described Route B as preserving the 2026-06-12 finding at the cost of a weaker match to the bottle. Drawn, that is wrong, and the reason is worth stating plainly.

Route B and the chosen bottle are photographic negatives of each other. Identical geometry, identical channel width and position, identical vertical wordmark, identical mono table with the same right-aligned unit column. The only difference is which way round the black and the board sit. That is not a weak match. It is a deliberate two-family system: the kit is the light member, the supplement is the dark member, and they are unmistakably the same brand because everything structural is shared.

It also resolves the tension instead of overriding it. The 2026-06-12 finding is about the object that carries the anxiety: a blood test you are nervous about opening. Warm white is the right answer for that. A supplement bottle on a bathroom shelf carries no such weight, so it can go dark. Route B lets each object be the right colour for its job while staying one system.

Where Route B is genuinely better

  • The credentials get the whole page. UKAS, the clinical sign-off and the IVD / CE / UKCA marks sit in black on clean board under a full-width rule, at full weight. On Route A they are squeezed onto a strip at the foot. The research called credentials the number-one trust lever, and Route B treats them like it.
  • The channel runs the full height, edge to edge. On Route A the band bounds it top and bottom; here the black spine runs the whole 152 mm and bleeds off both edges, which is closer to the label's own spine than Route A manages.
  • Far cheaper and safer to print. Route A is a large solid black area on board, which scuffs, shows handling and effectively requires matt lamination. Route B is a narrow black band plus hairlines. This is the difference between a lamination you must buy and one you may choose.
  • Better on a doorstep. A mostly-white box with one black spine reads as clinical post. A mostly-black box reads as something else, which is the concern the 2026-06-12 research raised in the first place.

Where Route A still wins

  • Shelf and thumbnail presence. Route A is louder at 200 px. For a kit that is mostly sold on a web page rather than a shelf, that matters less than it does for the bottle, but it is real.
  • Literal match. If you want the kit and the bottle to look like the same object rather than the same system, Route A gets closer.

Recommendation

Route B. It keeps the research decision, costs less to print, gives the credentials the space they earn, and still reads as one brand beside the bottle because the geometry and the table are identical. The negative-pair relationship is a stronger brand idea than a literal colour match, and it scales: any future light-format product joins the kit family, any dark-format product joins the supplement family, and nothing has to be redesigned to do it.

Unchanged, and still open

  • Dieline 179 × 152 mm. Front only is kit-specific; back and insert stay universal and the back keeps the white cutout for Vitall's lot and expiry. Untouched here.
  • CHECK The clinical sign-off line is carried verbatim from v5 and still needs re-confirming against the retired "GP-led report" differentiator.
  • CHECK Marker units against Vitall's report output. FAI is returned by the lab, not calculated by us, and reported without interpretation.
  • OPEN Warm white versus pure white. Both drawn. Still one call for the whole range.
  • OPEN QR fix from the /activate deprecation is a back-face item and is not addressed here.

Full-zoom check, and one round rejected

  • Every string verbatim across all three, including the UKAS line, the clinical sign-off line, the IVD / CE / UKCA marks and each REF code. The micro sign in µG/L survived, because the character was named in the prompt.
  • 🔴 The first Kit 1 and Kit 2 renders were rejected. The model re-laid-out the marker table so the unit column drifted away from the marker names: on Kit 1 the units floated to the top right entirely, on Kit 2 they sat one row above their marker. Every character was correct and the pairing was wrong, which on a medical panel is a real error rather than a cosmetic one. The rejected Kit 1 is kept in renders/higgsfield-test/ as the record.
  • The fix was to state the row rule explicitly: name and unit share a baseline, hairline rule underneath the pair, and every pair listed. Re-run, both correct. Kit 3 passed first time, which is the tell: its two-column table gave the model less room to reflow.
  • RULE When a generated pack image contains a table, check the pairing, not just the characters. Correct words in the wrong rows is the failure a spellcheck-style read misses.

The box reads right: slim letterbox carton, correct 179 × 152 face with a shallow 22 mm depth, matt uncoated board. The spine runs the full height and turns the corner onto the side, which is what it should do on a real sleeve.
