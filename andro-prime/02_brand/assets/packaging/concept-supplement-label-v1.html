Front faces — same product, same content, three treatments

Black spine carries the wordmark; the white field carries the data. The badge row from the template becomes three square keylines. Strongest shelf-standout of the three, and the spine reads at 15 mm on a shelf.

B · Monolith (template 5)

Full-bleed black, giant vertical name, nothing else. Closest to the site's black panels and the boldest of the three. Costs more to print well (large solid coverage shows every scuff) and the data column gets tight on a 50 mm bottle.

C · Clinical (template 4)

The template's minimalism, corrected to our system: left-aligned instead of centred, and the separation done with hairline rules instead of whitespace. Quietest, cheapest to print, weakest on a shelf next to shouty competitors.

Palette check against the kit sleeve — a conflict Keith needs to settle

A · Spine, in the kit sleeve's palette

Identical layout to A, repainted in the sleeve colours: ivory #F4F1EA and ink #141414 instead of #FFFFFF and #000000.

Why this panel exists

The three directions above are drawn in the brand's core palette: pure white and true black. The kit sleeve is not. On 2026-06-12 the sleeve direction was set to a warm-white ivory exterior on the research finding that black is the riskiest choice in a health frame, and the committed sleeve concepts use ivory #F4F1EA with ink #141414.

A customer receives the kit and the supplement inside the same month. They cannot be different whites. So one of two things has to be true, and it is a Keith call, not a design call:

  • Either the supplement labels move to the sleeve's ivory and ink, as drawn here, and the range reads as one system with the kit. This makes direction B unbuildable, because a warm-white brand does not have a full-bleed black SKU in it.
  • Or the 2026-06-12 warm-white finding is revisited, and the sleeve moves to pure white and true black with the labels. That reopens a research-led decision that was made deliberately and is already reflected in the printer brief.

The same open item is already logged against the sleeve as the "warm-white-vs-pure-white and ink-black-vs-#000" call. This is that same decision arriving a second time, from the supplement side. Settling it once now covers both.

Recommendation: settle it before choosing between A, B and C, because it removes B from the shortlist if ivory wins.

Back face — identical across all three directions

Back face · shared

What the back face is doing

  • Ashwagandha appears here and only here. It is a legally required ingredient declaration, not copy. It stays off the front face, out of the claim line, and out of everything else per the silent-ingredient rule.
  • One claim, verbatim. Zinc's EFSA wording is the only health claim on the pack. Vitamin D3 and B12 have approved claims too but adding them crowds the face and widens the ASA surface for no gain.
  • The dashed box is not artwork. The 12-week duration line comes from Ewa's 2026-08-02 note on the liver-safety caution and has never been approved. It is drawn so it cannot be mistaken for signed-off copy.
  • Ingredient list is indicative. Excipients come from the manufacturer's declaration, not from us. Silicon dioxide and rice flour are placeholders chosen to avoid naming magnesium.

Notes

What each direction takes from its template

  • A · Spine, from template 1. The full-height dark band and the badge strip. Band recoloured to true black, badges redrawn as square keylines, brand name set vertically in Inter Black at natural letter-spacing.
  • B · Monolith, from template 5. The giant vertical product name on a flat field. The template's cream and coral faces are dropped: the range differentiates by the mono data column, never by colour.
  • C · Clinical, from template 4. The empty, quiet field. Corrected from centred-with-whitespace to left-aligned-with-rules, because the brand separates with structural black lines rather than air.

What all three share

  • Black and white only. No accent, no gradient, no shadow, no rounded corners, square linecaps.
  • Outlined AP mark, which is the sanctioned large-format variant. The solid mark is not used at this size.
  • Inter Black for the brand, Merriweather Bold for the product name (matching the kit sleeve, where "Testosterone" is set the same way), JetBrains Mono for every number and label.
  • The dose table is the hero content on all three. That is the argument the brand actually makes: this product exists because a marker in your kit came back low.
  • No em dashes anywhere in the pack copy.

Compliance state of this artwork

  • OK Zinc claim is the exact EFSA wording. Nothing on the pack says the product raises testosterone.
  • OK Ashwagandha appears only in the ingredient declaration and the nutrition table, never in the claim line or the front face.
  • OPEN The 12-week duration line is an Ewa gate and is drawn dashed. It ships only if she signs the wording.
  • OPEN Excipients are indicative until the manufacturer supplies the real declaration.
  • OPEN Collagen formulation is unresolved (UC-II versus hydrolysed lane), so that face shows the Vitamin C claim only.
  • OPEN Omega-3 is drawn as an illustration of range fit. It is not committed and the lab feasibility question is unanswered.

Open before any print

  • Pick one direction. They are not mixable across a range: a black bottle next to a white one reads as two brands, not one system.
  • Confirm the real bottle. This is drawn at a 75 × 90 mm front face on a wrap label. The manufacturer's bottle dimensions decide the true dieline, exactly as Vitall's dieline decided the sleeve.
  • Warm white or pure white, and ink black or true black. Same call left open on the kit sleeve. Whatever is chosen has to match the sleeve, since a customer sees both in the same month.
  • Finish. Matt laminate versus uncoated. Direction B needs the better finish to survive handling.
