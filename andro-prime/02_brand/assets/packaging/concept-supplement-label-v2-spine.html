What changed from the concept

  • Real proportions. The concept was drawn at a portrait 75 × 90 mm face, which a round bottle cannot carry. This is the true front assembly of a wrap label: a 20 mm spine plus a 78 mm field, 90 mm tall.
  • The spine got its job back. At 20 mm and 90 mm tall the wordmark reads across a shop floor, which is the whole argument for template 1.
  • Badges moved down and lost one. Two keylines and the URL share the bottom rule. Three badges plus a URL does not fit at real size without dropping below legible type.
  • Type sizes are now legal sizes, not visual ones. See the spec table at the foot.

Why the back is two columns

Mandatory particulars have a legal minimum letter height, and at that size a single column of advisory text runs off the panel. Splitting ingredients and directions from the advisory buys the height back and leaves room for a real barcode.

Ashwagandha is here and only here, in the nutrient table and the ingredient list, because the law requires the declaration. It is absent from the front, the claim line and every badge.

The dashed block is Ewa's gate, not artwork. The 12-week line comes from her 2026-08-02 liver-safety note and the wording has never been approved.

Ink, stock, finish

  • One colour. Every face is black ink on stock. No spot, no process, no varnish build. That is the cheapest supplement label a printer can quote, and it is a direct consequence of the brand having no accent colour.
  • Stock decides the palette, not the ink. Pure white artwork on a white self-adhesive stock, or the same artwork on a natural/ivory stock at #F4F1EA. Same plates either way.
  • Finish: matt laminate recommended over uncoated. Solid black at 20 mm shows every fingerprint on an uncoated stock, and this label lives in a bathroom.
  • Fonts supplied outlined: Inter Black for the wordmark, Merriweather Bold for the product name, JetBrains Mono for every number and label. Same three as the kit sleeve.

Type sizes and the legal floor

Mandatory particulars on a food supplement have a minimum letter height under the food information rules: an x-height of 1.2 mm where the largest surface is 80 cm² or more, which this container is. Every mandatory item on the back is set at 2.2 mm type, giving an x-height of roughly 1.2 mm in Inter. That is why the back is two columns rather than one: at legal size, one column does not fit.

CHECK This is drawn to the 1.2 mm floor as read from the regulation. Have the printer or a labelling consultant confirm it against the final artwork before plates. Getting this wrong is a relabel, not an amendment.

Flags

  • DECIDE Palette. White or ivory. Both are drawn above. The kit sleeve is already ivory, so ivory is the consistent answer unless the sleeve moves.
  • CONFIRM The bottle. Drawn on a 250 cc bottle, which is deliberately oversized for 60 capsules: it buys the label panel height the dose table needs. If the manufacturer pushes to a 150 cc bottle, the panel drops to about 65 mm and the dose table has to move to the back, which removes the reason this direction was chosen.
  • EWA 12-week duration line. Drawn dashed. Ships only with her wording.
  • EWA Omega-3 heart claim. Drawn, but the framing is unapproved and the product is uncommitted.
  • OPEN Excipients. Rice flour and silicon dioxide are placeholders. The real list comes from the manufacturer.
  • OPEN Collagen formula. The UC-II versus hydrolysed lane is undecided, so that face carries one real row.

What is not in this file

Net quantity, best-before, storage instructions, country of origin and the responsible business address are all mandatory and all live in the printer-applied or fine-print zones drawn dashed. They are placeholders here because they depend on the manufacturer and the final fill. Do not treat this as a complete legal artwork.
