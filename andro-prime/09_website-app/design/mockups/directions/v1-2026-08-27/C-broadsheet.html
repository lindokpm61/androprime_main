Direction C: Broadsheet

The idea: the literal delivery of the brief you said you originally wrote. Not "flat and clean", which is what got built, but editorial: a masthead, a standfirst, a drop cap, a two-column article body, a figure with a numbered caption, a rule-bounded pull band, and a product spread. The page argues like a piece of writing rather than like a landing page, which suits a brand whose whole proposition is "we explain, we do not sell you the answer".

Dials

DESIGN_VARIANCE 7   MOTION_INTENSITY 4   VISUAL_DENSITY 3. The airiest of the three. Whitespace is the material.

How it is deliberately not A or B

  • Third palette family. A spends the warm packaging bone, B spends cold smoke, C is pure monochrome on cool paper. No direction repeats another's palette, per the skill's rotation rule.
  • Third serif. Source Serif 4, the serif of scientific and medical publishing, which is the exact register. Not A's Newsreader, and neither of the two banned LLM-default display serifs.
  • Different layout families throughout: masthead, centred manifesto hero, in-column figure, two-column article body, product spread. Nothing is reused from A or B.
  • The readout is a figure, not a hero. In A it sits beside the headline; in B it is the hero; here it sits inside the argument like a chart in an article, with Fig. 1 and a caption. Same component, three different jobs.

The one centred hero

Centred heroes are avoided above DESIGN_VARIANCE 4, with a documented exception for editorial and manifesto briefs where the message is the design. That is exactly this page, so the exception is being taken deliberately rather than by default.

Compliance

Zero em dashes. No "diagnose", "treat", "cure"; the caption says "Nothing here is a diagnosis". Conflict-free receipt verbatim. No membership sold or priced. No EFSA-claim ingredients, no ashwagandha, no TRT availability. All values and band positions from thresholds.md. Not pre-flighted yet.
