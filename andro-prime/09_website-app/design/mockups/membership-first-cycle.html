What the difference means

A reference range answers one question: where do most men sit? An action cutoff answers a different one: below what number should someone do something? They are built for different purposes, so they give different numbers. You are inside the first and below the second.

Both numbers, or the claim collapses

Hiding the lab range would be the "moving the goalposts" charge made against private clinics, committed invisibly. It also cannot be hidden: the laboratory returns it with every result and the customer has a right of access to it. Showing it costs nothing and buys the whole argument.

Four of nine markers have a story here

Testosterone (12 against the lab's 8.64), active B12 (NICE NG239 bands against the assay's single 37.5 cut) and ferritin (30 against the older 15) are where we hold a tighter line. Vitamin D matches the assay at both ends. SHBG and free testosterone use whatever the lab returns. On the markers where we agree, saying so is itself reassuring.

The loop cannot score his health, so it does not try

ZOE can return a score the moment you photograph a meal because they own a model to score it against. We do not. What we can score honestly is adherence to the one thing he said he would change, and the retest is the payoff. That makes the dated retest load-bearing rather than decorative: without it the streak has nothing to pay out and the loop decays around week three.

Log only what connects to the marker being moved

An earlier draft of this row asked for the capsule, sleep, sun and steps. Sleep and steps do not move vitamin D. They were there because they feel healthy, which means asking a man to report data the app cannot act on, and that is the fastest way to kill a logging habit.

Three taps, all connected to this marker: the capsule he is taking, the daylight that is the other source, and the symptom that sent him for the test. A member moving ferritin would see different inputs. The loop scopes itself, which keeps it short.

Where the data actually comes from: he types it

No wearable knows whether he swallowed a capsule, and adherence is the load-bearing input. The web-app decision also puts Apple Health permanently out of reach, since HealthKit is native-only and Google's equivalent is too, so the step count already sitting in his phone cannot be read.

What a web backend can reach is the vendor APIs: Oura, Whoop, Fitbit, Garmin and Withings all expose server-side OAuth. That covers ring and watch owners, who are the tracking-minded minority. Worth building when members ask for it, not before, and then one integration chosen by what they actually own.

Energy is logged, never explained

A member seeing his own symptom trend is not a claim. The app must never connect the two for him: "your energy improved because your vitamin D rose" is an interpretation we cannot make in Phase 0. It shows both lines and says nothing about the relationship.

Vitamin D, not testosterone, is what demonstrates the app

Testosterone does not move on wellness supplements, which is exactly why kit 1 is not sold as a prove-it bundle. Vitamin D, active B12, hs-CRP and ferritin move in 8 to 16 weeks. The brand leads on the marker least able to show the product working, and this screen is where that tension is visible.

A benefit, never a credit

The earlier version of this screen offered GBP 99 of testing credit. Credit is a thing the member owns, which makes it a balance-sheet liability, needs a ledger to issue and expire, and leaves a genuine argument at cancellation with a man who paid three months and believes he is still owed a kit. Under the Consumer Rights Act that ambiguity is read against us.

Same commercial outcome, different object: membership includes the retest on its date, and the condition is being a member on that date. Gym logic, not gift-voucher logic. It removes the liability, the ledger and the expiry term all at once.

Why GBP 47 rather than GBP 39

GBP 47 nets GBP 39.17 once VAT applies, so the sticker never has to change on crossing the GBP 90,000 threshold. Below it the whole GBP 47 is kept. Pricing at GBP 39 and adding VAT later forces either a visible 20% rise or a 17% cut to the recurring line. The cost is a 20% higher ask on the highest-variance screen in the business, and that is a judgement, not a measurement.

The decline option is deliberate, and it is the law

Under UK GDPR a person has a right of access to their own health data. Gate the interpretation, the plan and the trend. Never the numbers.

Why does vitamin D take so long to shift?

Stored vitamin D turns over slowly, so blood levels respond over weeks rather than days. Most people testing after supplementation see a meaningful change somewhere between eight and sixteen weeks, which is why retesting sooner usually tells you very little.

The clinician leg is real, and it is narrower than it looks

Swapping Dr Lindo for a different qualified professional does not move the regulatory line, because the regulated thing is the act, not the person. Per-customer interpretation of an individual's results is post-CQC, whoever performs it.

What works now is the published answer: a member asks, a clinician answers generally, every member sees it. Health information rather than clinical advice. It is already funded at GBP 150 a month in the model as clinician content. Never call it a care team, a consultation, or a review.

Supplements are a shop, not a subscription

Neither Function nor Superpower include supplements in their membership; both run a member-priced marketplace on top of a fee that already covers a lot more than ours does. Selling at member price rather than including them keeps the recurring line near pure margin and takes stock, dispatch cadence and the 3PL out of the membership entirely.

His retest is annual, not at day 90

There is no number he is trying to move, so a 90 day retest would be testing for the sake of a schedule. That is the cadence the BMJ criticised at Numan against national minimum retesting intervals, and it is also the version that destroys the margin: four test events a year against GBP 564 of subscription takes gross from roughly 87% to the high 50s.

The clinical answer and the financial answer agree, which is rare enough to design around.

How common is this? Nobody knows, and the mock does not claim to

An earlier draft of this screen called him the majority. That came from a figure of 93%, which was a non-referral rate for a different company across a catalogue of mostly narrow tests, and it does not transfer here. On nine markers the statistical all-clear share sits in the 60s; our markers are partly derived, which pushes it up, and our buyers are self-selected and symptomatic, which pushes it back down.

With three orders on the books it is unmeasurable. It needs about thirty results, and until then this is a case to serve rather than a cohort to size.

A vertical test finds nothing far more often than an agnostic panel does

Reference intervals are set at the central 95% of a reference population, so each marker flags about 5% of well men by construction. Testosterone alone is roughly three independent dimensions and finds something in about one man in five. Nine markers across two axes finds something in more than one in three. Metabolic and thyroid panels push it further.

That is the argument for the agnostic panel rather than a problem with it: testing one thing is how you miss it. It is also why Numan needs "fear nothing" framing, because a single-vertical test usually comes back clear.

The line that keeps it honest: chosen, not bundled

Numan ran the other version, twenty-one markers with a refund if everything was normal, and the ASA found only 4 to 10% of users qualified. The difference is not the marker count, it is who decided. A panel a man elects because he has a question is a product. Markers added to one test to raise the hit rate is the incumbent playbook.

This is still the harder sell

He is being asked for GBP 47 a month against a promise that matters in twelve months. The loop has no target and the retest is a year away. Every comparator solves this with something that pays out on day one: ZOE with a daily score, Function and Superpower with a person.

The two-range card is the best day-one asset in the file, which is why it appears on his screen too. It is worth treating as the answer to the cold-start problem rather than as a compliance nicety.

The free androgen index is drawn faithfully

Hollow marker, no band, no conclusion. Kit 3 returns it from the lab and CA-034 K1 says we do not interpret it. A mock that quietly banded it would be designing something that cannot ship.

The upgrade path survives the reframing intact

The commercial virtue of the old credit model was that gross rises with every upgrade, because the member tops up rather than taking a fixed giveaway. That still holds. What changed is the object: he is not spending a balance he owns, he is putting his included test toward a bigger panel and paying the difference.

Two separate retests would be two finger-pricks and more lab cost. One nine-marker sample is cheaper for him and better for margin, which is rare enough to design around.

Marker first, kit second

Nine markers from two purchases read as one picture. That is the only way this scales past two kits, and it is the architecture the whole pre-vertical position depends on: the app has no opinion about which vertical a number belongs to.

Only our own kits feed the trend

We do not ingest results from other providers. A number typed in from a photo has no assay identity and no collection time, testosterone needs a morning sample, and a line drawn between two different assays is an artefact that looks like information. The refusal is a positioning asset: we only chart what we can stand behind.

What this still does not show

Deliberate omissions, so the mock is not read as a spec:

  • The GP-referral path. A red-flag result routes out of this flow entirely: no plan, no shop, no retest countdown.
  • Wearable connect. Everything here is manual entry. Oura, Whoop, Fitbit, Garmin and Withings are reachable from a web backend over OAuth and would fill the passive inputs; Apple Health and Google Health Connect are not reachable at all without a native app. Deliberately unbuilt until members ask.
  • Failure states. Sample rejected, retest declined, payment failed, streak broken. Each is a real moment and none is designed.
  • The demo. The free layer is the published articles plus a login showing a sample result. That login is what marketing points at, and it is not drawn here.
  • Month 13 onward. A second annual cycle for a member who has never had anything wrong is the real retention test, and it is the next screen worth drawing.
