One finding, which is the normal case

Across four unrelated markers, roughly three quarters of people get at least one flag, and in winter vitamin D is usually the one. A member with nothing to fix is the minority, and a harder sell.

The plan is the day 14 to 30 event

Days 0 to 14 have a kit arriving and results landing. Days 14 to 30 have nothing, and that is the fortnight immediately before the first charge. Three actions with a visible streak is what fills it.

The chart is about behaviour, not blood

Markers move over months, so nothing here claims the blood changed. It shows what the member did, which is the only thing that can honestly move in 22 days.

It sells the retest, not the software

Three payments of £49 more than cover a £58.50 test event, so the promise and the money genuinely line up. "Keep going" beats "subscribe" because the member is mid-effort, not mid-trial.

The three numbers are loss, not features

30 days logged, a 16 day streak, one marker to move. What they lose by leaving is the thread, and the thread is the only thing here they cannot get anywhere else.

The decline option is deliberate, and it is the law

ZOE require an active subscription to see test results and take criticism for it. Under UK GDPR a person has a right of access to their own health data. Gate the interpretation, the plan and the trend. Never the numbers.

£49 is the 88% margin price

With one test event included per year at £58.50, £49 a month lands near 88% gross and £59 lands at 90%. Below about £39 the test cost eats the model.

Vitamin D is the only number you are trying to move. The other eight are your baseline.

Marker first, kit second

The panel a number came from is metadata on the row, not a tab the member has to navigate. Nine markers from two purchases read as one picture, which is the only way this scales past two kits.

Nine markers, one finding, and that is fine

Eight green rows are not a failure state, they are the baseline the next test is measured against. The screen has to make "nothing wrong" feel like an asset rather than a wasted purchase, because that is most people most of the time.

FAI is reported, not interpreted

Drawn faithfully: a hollow marker, no band, no conclusion, no action. Kit 1 returns it from the lab and CA-034 K1 says we do not interpret it. A mockup that quietly banded it would be designing something you cannot ship.

The credit answers the multi-kit retest cleanly

Fixed £99 of value, spent on whichever panel covers what they actually track. A member on both axes puts it toward Kit 3 and tops up £80.

And the top-up is better for you than the free option

Two separate retests would be £117 of lab cost and two finger-pricks. Kit 3 covers all nine for £98 on one sample, and the member pays £80 toward it. The cheapest choice for them is also the best one for your margin, which is rare enough to be worth designing around.

This is the upsell you were missing

Without the app, a second kit is another one-off report. With it, a second kit adds markers to a system already in daily use. The app makes the next kit worth buying, and the next kit makes the app worth keeping.

What this still does not show

Deliberate omissions, so the mock is not read as a spec:

  • The GP-referral path. A red-flag result routes out of this flow entirely: no plan, no shop, no retest countdown.
  • The all-clear member. Four green markers and a 6 to 12 month retest produces a much weaker version of every screen here, and it needs its own answer rather than this one with the numbers changed.
  • Wearable connect. Sleep is drawn as manual entry. An Oura or Whoop connection fills it passively and is the version that does not decay.
  • Day 90 itself. The second result landing is the payoff the whole cycle is built toward, and it is the next screen worth drawing.
  • Failure states. Sample rejected, retest declined, payment failed, streak broken. Each one is a real moment and none is designed.
