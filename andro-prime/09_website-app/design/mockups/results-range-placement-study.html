What this means

Your SHBG is within the normal range. It is binding and releasing testosterone at a typical rate, so your free testosterone should be in proportion to your total.

The evidence

Sex Hormone Binding Globulin is made in the liver and carries testosterone through the bloodstream. Only the free fraction can enter cells and have an effect.

What it does well

Nothing thin is load-bearing. The declaration is a full-width statement rather than a chip, and the change of surface backs it up, so camouflage stops being the risk. B's explanation column survives intact.

The risk

Two disclaimers in a row, the bar and then "we recommend nothing", can read as protesting too much. If one has to go, the bar stays and the recommend strip goes quiet.

Click goes to

Product page, same as the others. The card is the target and already carries the claim, so the page adds the marker mapping, the full label and the subscribe control.

Reading these

  • Product page, not straight to checkout. The marker mapping and the full ingredient label live on the product page, and they are what make the sale defensible. Skipping to checkout removes the only screen that explains why the product exists. Put a subscribe control on the product page instead, so it is still two clicks.
  • The whole card is the target, not just the price. Pack shot, name, dose and claim are all inside one link, with hover lifting the card and the arrow going solid.
  • These packs are CSS, not renders. Good enough to judge placement and size. For build, the three would be shot the same way as the Daily Stack flat mail packer render already in 02_brand.
  • Shown on a normal result on purpose. Total testosterone 16.8, everything in range. Under 12, or hs-CRP over 10, the page routes to a GP and the range does not render in any of the three.
  • Fixed order in all three: D3, B12, Zinc, whatever came back low. The moment order or contents respond to the result, all three break the rule that no result changes what we offer.
  • No zinc dose is printed anywhere, on purpose. The spec is 25 mg gluconate, the only Nutribl SKU is 15 mg citrate, and Ewa's 2026-08-02 decline of added copper was explicitly conditional on 25 mg being low enough. Nothing goes onto artwork until she answers. Same reason there is no ashwagandha on the shelf.
  • No prices either. The 9.95 ladder in the first study assumed the separate-bottles architecture, still logged open in 04_products/STATE.md after the Nutribl finding. The only live price today is a 34.95 Daily Stack in pricing.ts. Placement can be decided without settling that; printing a number cannot.
  • D's recommend strip uses a ghost link, and B's has been changed to match. A solid black "See the range" button sitting under the words "we recommend nothing" was the copy disclaiming while the strongest element on the strip recommended.
The pick

D. Selling is not the risk here and nobody objects to a shop. The risk is commerce that reads as advice derived from a man's blood, and the way that happens is camouflage, not prominence. B is the most tasteful and therefore the most confusable, because it works by borrowing the report's own grammar. C fixes that by leaving the grammar behind, and loses the marker mapping and the "why it never changes" column doing it.

D keeps what B was right about and removes the thing that made it risky. The declaration does the work the chip could not carry, and the surface change means a fast reader who never reads the bar still sees a different kind of block. When this goes to Ewa, the sentence to point at is "the shelf does not read your results, so it cannot respond to them", which is the claim CA-026 clause 2 makes, said in the place the customer is actually standing.
