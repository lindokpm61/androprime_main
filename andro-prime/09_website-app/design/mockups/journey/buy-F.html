🔴 APPROVED CONSENT COPY, VERSION-LOCKED, AND IT IS NOT WHERE THE INVENTORY SAYS IT IS. The sentence in the amber box is the Article 9(2)(a) health-data consent, approved as CA-018 (Ewa and Keith) and pinned to HEALTH_PROCESSING_CONSENT_VERSION, currently 2026-06-23-v1. The source comment is explicit: it is captured here, at the point of purchase, "so it is freely given as part of deciding to buy", it is required to proceed to payment, and "any wording change needs a new version string plus a fresh CA record". That puts it outside what a redesign may touch: not reworded, not shortened to fit, not split across two lines for rhythm. `design/journey-inventory.md` records CA-018 against `/auth/consent` in the Account stage. It is on this form too, and this is the one a buyer actually meets, because it gates payment.

Why the consent row is the only thing on this page wearing the accent. It is the single required control, it is the one thing that blocks the button, and on the live page it is styled the same as everything else in the card. F spends its one marketing accent where the page is pointing, and on a two-field form the thing being pointed at is the box a buyer has to tick. The segmented control gets the same amber on hover, which is the idiom already settled on the quiz options.

The 18 gate is enforced twice and stated three times. The date input carries a `max` of today minus 18 years, `isAtLeast18()` re-checks on submit, and "18+ only" appears in the reassurance strip. Drawn as it is: a redesign that replaced the native date input with a prettier custom control would drop the first of those three without anyone noticing, because the other two would still be there.

The button is disabled while the checkout session is created, and the label changes rather than a spinner appearing. That is the whole of the motion budget this frame spends, and it is the correct place to spend it: the wait is real, it is between a click and a redirect off-site, and a label that says what is happening survives reduced motion where a spinner does not.

The errors are drawn as a stack so the set can be read at once. On the page only one appears at a time, in the same position above the button, because the form returns on the first failure rather than validating everything and listing the results. That is a real behaviour and the rebuild should keep it or change it deliberately: on a three-field form, first-failure is arguably right, and it is also the reason a buyer who has missed two things gets told about them one at a time.

THREE RENDERABLE STATES, NOT FOUR, AND THE FOURTH COMBINATION IS THE INTERESTING ONE. Signed-in and reference-resolved are two binaries, but they are not independent: resolving a reference requires a signed-in user, so "not signed in with a reference" cannot occur. What exists instead is a redirect: a signed-out arrival carrying a `session_id` is sent to `/auth/post-checkout` and renders nothing at all. That redirect has a loop guard with a scar: `post_checkout=1` is stamped on the way back, and the source comment records that the route used to drop `session_id`, so a first-time buyer could never resolve a reference and a failed sign-in bounced him between two routes forever. A frame cannot draw a redirect, but an inventory that counts screens will miss it, so it is written down here.

State 2 is drawn in the neutral tray rather than the accent. The accent marks a reference a customer can act on; there is nothing to act on in the fallback, and dressing an apology in the same amber as the real thing is how a design starts lying about which state is which.
