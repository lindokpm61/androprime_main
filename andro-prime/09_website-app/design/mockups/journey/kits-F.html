The data

Five numbers.
The full testosterone picture.

Biomarker 01

Total testosterone

The total amount of testosterone in your blood. Your baseline. The number most GPs test, if they test anything at all.

Biomarker 02

SHBG

Sex Hormone Binding Globulin. It binds to testosterone and makes it unusable. High SHBG means your total T might look fine on paper while you still feel terrible.

Biomarker 03

Free androgen index

The ratio of total testosterone to SHBG, expressed as a percentage. A more sensitive indicator of testosterone availability than Total T alone, especially when SHBG is elevated.

Biomarker 04

Albumin

The main carrier protein in your blood. Albumin-bound testosterone is considered weakly bioavailable. Testing it allows accurate calculation of your Free Testosterone. Without it, the number is an estimate.

Biomarker 05

Free testosterone

The testosterone your body can actually use. Calculated from your Total T, SHBG, and Albumin. This is the number that matters most for how you feel day to day.

The data

A blood test for tiredness.
Four markers, four answers.

Biomarker 01

Vitamin D

Most UK men are deficient between October and March. Low vitamin D directly affects muscle function, recovery speed, and energy. You can't tell from how you feel. You can only tell from your blood.

Biomarker 02

Active B12

Holotranscobalamin: the form of B12 your cells can actually absorb. Standard NHS B12 tests often miss deficiency. Active B12 catches it early. Low levels affect energy, nerve function, and how quickly you recover between sessions.

Biomarker 03

hs-CRP (inflammation)

A high-sensitivity inflammation marker. If this is elevated, your body is dealing with inflammation it isn't clearing. In active men, this is often linked to joint and connective tissue stress.

Biomarker 04

Ferritin

Your iron stores. Low ferritin is one of the most common and most overlooked causes of fatigue in men. If your energy has dropped off a cliff, this is often why.
