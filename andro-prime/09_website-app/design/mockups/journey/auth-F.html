🔴 THE INVENTORY SAID FIVE AUTH FRAMES. FOUR OF THE FIVE ARE THIS CARD. `components/auth/AuthCard.tsx`, 177 lines, with `mode = login | signup | reset | link`. The four routes are 25-line wrappers passing in a title, a standfirst and a server action. Here it says five frames and there is one component. Both readings came from route names rather than files, and they were wrong in opposite directions, which is why no rule of thumb about how many frames a stage "usually" holds would have caught either.

Five differences and that is the whole of it: the heading, the standfirst, which fields render, the submit label, and which of the four cross-links appear (each mode hides its own). The struck-through rows are what the mode does NOT render. Drawn as a row rather than four full cards, for the same reason the six subscription status badges and the nine order statuses were drawn as rows: they are variants of one screen, not four screens, and drawing them as four would repeat the counting error that put five frames on the board.

🔴 18+ IS COLLECTED THREE TIMES AND ENFORCED TWICE. `/checkout/details` takes a date of birth with a `max` attribute AND an `isAtLeast18()` re-check on submit. `/auth/consent` takes an `age` with `min={18}` and required. `/auth/signup` takes an `age` with `min={18}` and no `required` attribute at all, so the same eligibility fact is mandatory in two places and optional in the third. Drawn exactly as it ships. It is a behaviour question rather than a design one, and it belongs to Keith.

Both banner strings arrive in the URL rather than from the component, so the text is whatever the redirecting action put there. The two examples above are illustrative of the SHAPE and are marked as such: they are not transcribed from a fixed list, because there is not one in the component. Anything the rebuild wants to standardise about this wording is a copy exercise that has to start from the server actions in lib/auth/actions.ts, not from here.

The message banner takes the accent and the error takes the neutral rule, which is the opposite of the obvious choice. An error on an auth form is almost always something the reader already knows (a wrong password), and it needs to be legible rather than loud; a message is usually the thing that just happened elsewhere and explains why they are looking at this screen ("check your email"), which is the thing worth pointing at. Status colour is not available here anyway, so the question is only which of the two gets the one accent.

🔴 THIS ROUTE CARRIES NO HEALTH-DATA CONSENT, AND THE INVENTORY SAID IT WAS THE ONE WITH COMPLIANCE WEIGHT. The line read: "Consent is the one with compliance weight (health-data processing, CA-018) and its wording is approved copy that the redesign must not disturb." Both halves are wrong. The route asks an age for 18+ eligibility and offers a marketing opt-in. There is no Article 9(2)(a) wording on it, no CA-018 sentence, nothing version-locked. The health-data consent is on `/checkout/details`, gating payment, found while drawing the Buy stage. That line had already been corrected to say the copy here was UNVERIFIED and had to be read rather than inherited. It was read, and this is what it says. A note that admits it does not know is worth more than a note that is confidently wrong, because the second one gets quoted.

The route name is the second mismatch in this set. `/faq` is not an FAQ and `/auth/consent` is not the health consent. Both names describe what a reader would assume rather than what the page holds, and both were taken at face value by an inventory built from route names. The marketing opt-in here is also the second copy of a sentence that already exists on `/auth/signup`, word for word: fifth member of the duplication pattern.
