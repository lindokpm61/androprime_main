The myth of the “normal” range

Your GP said your testosterone was normal. That is not the same as good, and the difference is the whole point of this article.

KA Written by Keith Antony Founder, Andro Prime
EL Reviewed by Dr Ewa Lindo GMC-registered GP · reviewed 12 Aug 2026

The NHS reference range for testosterone in men is roughly 8 to 30 nmol/L. That range was built to identify clinical hypogonadism: the level at which a man is medically deficient. A man at 8.5 nmol/L and a man at 24 nmol/L both get the same result from their GP: normal.

They are not the same. Not in how they feel.

The range exists to identify illness, not to optimise performance. It answers one question, correctly, and it is the wrong question for a man who feels flat and wants to know why.

Worth knowing. A reference range is a population statistic, not a target. It is built so that 95% of a reference population falls inside it, which means it moves with the population it was drawn from.
What the evidence says

Research consistently shows that men with testosterone in the lower third of the normal range report significantly higher rates of fatigue, reduced libido, slower recovery and mood changes than men in the upper third, despite both being technically not ill.

The evidence

The Society for Endocrinology and the Association for Clinical Biochemistry describe their figures as action cutoffs rather than reference ranges: below 8 nmol/L likely hypogonadism, 8 to 12 equivocal, above 12 unlikely. Cutoffs for a decision, not a definition of good.

Technically normal. Functionally below par. Those are two different sentences about the same number.

Clinical insight

A single testosterone reading is a snapshot taken at one hour of one day. Levels move diurnally and seasonally, which is why a borderline result is a reason to retest rather than a reason to conclude.

This article is about how a range is built and read. It is not a substitute for a conversation with your GP, and no number on this page is a diagnosis.

🔴 ONE RUNG COLLAPSED ON THE FIRST RENDER OF THIS SHEET, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE RISK IN MINIATURE. Note (02) and PublishedEvidence (05) were drawn with the same ground and the same 3px rule, so they were one rung wearing two names. On the live blog they are 4px and 8px, deliberately far apart, because one is an aside the reader may skip and the other is somebody else's claim. Fixed by raising the rule to 5px and marking the citation foot with the accent, so the piece is identified by the thing that makes it different. It was caught by looking at the render rather than the markup, which is the only way this class of error is ever caught: the source was symmetric and reasonable, and the eye is what notices two rungs landing in the same place.

🔴 THE TWO SYSTEMS BUY THEIR RUNGS WITH DIFFERENT CURRENCY, AND THAT IS THE REAL CHOICE. The live blog spends border weight and one hard shadow: 2px, 4px, 6px, 8px, plus an 8px offset block. F spends ground: nothing, sunk, core, tray, inverted, plus one accent. Border weight is a fine ladder and it has a ceiling, because 10px is not a fourth of 8px and the eye stops counting. Ground has more rungs available and reads faster, since a reader identifies a surface before measuring an edge.

Two things F genuinely does NOT have, stated plainly rather than glossed. The dot pattern, which the live blog uses as a texture behind the headline and in the corner of the CTA, has no F equivalent and is not reproduced here; F's grain is a global film, not a placeable motif. And the hard offset block-shadow is not just a shadow, it is a statement of flatness that F's ambient shadow contradicts by design. If either is load-bearing for the blog's character, F cannot have it without stopping being F, and that is a legitimate reason to keep `blog-skin.css`. It is a real trade and this frame is not going to pretend otherwise.

What F gains, since the trade runs both ways. The live blog has no dark mode at all and cannot easily get one: the cream ground and the hard black shadow are both light-only decisions. Every rung above is drawn in both themes on this page. For a blog that is read on phones at night, that is not a small thing.

WHAT THIS FILE ASKS FOR, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT CLAIM. It does not overturn the 2026-08-27 ruling; that stands until Keith rules again. What it does is remove the reason the first draft lost. The verdict was "a lot of detail missing ... I don't know if you can capture", and the detail turned out to be nameable: twelve editorial components and a four-step emphasis ladder. All twelve are drawn, the ladder is drawn against the live one, and the two things F genuinely cannot reproduce are named rather than hidden.

The honest summary of the trade, in three lines. F gains a deeper ladder built on ground rather than border weight, and a real dark mode the live blog cannot easily have. F loses the dot pattern and the hard offset block-shadow, and the second of those is a statement of flatness that F contradicts by design. F is neutral on measure, rhythm and the tail, which translate cleanly. If the block-shadow is the blog's character rather than its decoration, keeping `blog-skin.css` remains the right answer and this file is the record of a fair second attempt.

One thing to fix either way, independent of the ruling. `blog-skin.css` is a written, namespaced, deliberate exception to two brand non-negotiables, and it is documented only in its own header. It is not referenced in `02_brand`. Whichever way the blog goes, an exception to a non-negotiable should be recorded where the non-negotiable lives, or the next reader meets the rule without the exception.
