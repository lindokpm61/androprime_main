03The white space

Not a product gap. A positioning gap between two camps that structurally cannot enter it.

Interpretation, tracking and trust dashboards are already owned by funded incumbents. The empty square is positional: a men's brand with no financial incentive to find something wrong with you. Tap each square.

The map has two axes: does the brand speak to men, and does it profit when your result is bad? Every serious competitor sits in one of the two occupied squares. Tap them to see why each is trapped there.

The claim, and why it holds
Camp 1 can't follow: the claim costs them their business model. Camp 2 won't: it costs them their audience.

Camp 1 can't. For Numan to say "we won't sell you testosterone," it would have to abandon its core revenue line. The claim is only available to a company whose economics don't depend on treatment. Ours don't: we sell the test, the retest, and supplements offered regardless of result, so a clean result costs us nothing.

Camp 2 won't. Thriva could say "conflict-free" tomorrow, but can't become a men's brand without fragmenting a general-population business, and "conflict-free" is only a weapon when pointed at the treatment funnels, a fight the generalists have no reason to start.

We already own the hardest part. Low-T-to-GP-with-no-upsell is a locked constraint, live in the classifier today. Most companies would have to change their business model to make this claim. We just have to say it.

And the mainstream buyer confirmed the square exists (21 Jul research). Anti-upsell anger is not forum ideology: undisclosed paid follow-up tests and subscription traps are verified mainstream complaint clusters at Numan, including an ASA ruling against its "fear nothing" ad for burying the subscription. The nuance that shapes our copy: the mainstream version is billing distrust, not anti-TRT ideology. He isn't angry they sell testosterone; he's angry he got charged for things he didn't agree to. So the mass-market claim leads with the wallet, not the industry.

04Capture, then dominate

Claim it. Prove it. Ambush the distrust moments. Package for it.

Move 1 · Claim

Say the sentence, in the right order

Speed earns the click, honesty earns the trust. Sentence one: 5 minutes, no GP needed, results in days. Sentence two: one price, nothing hidden, and if your testosterone is low we refer you to a GP; we earn the same whether you're fine or not. The sharper industry version ("no reason to sell you testosterone") is for press, comparisons and GEO, where it travels furthest.

Move 2 · Prove

Show the receipts

Claims are copy; positions are receipts. Ours are already built, just hidden in the product. No competitor can copy that section of the site without lying. See the list below.

Move 3 · Ambush

Own the distrust moments

The buyer is a man who suspects low T, has seen the TRT-clinic ads, and doesn't trust them. Own "is [brand] legit", "do I actually need TRT", "GP refused testosterone test", "normal range but still tired". The position is its own soundbite: "the one that doesn't sell testosterone" survives being compressed into an AI answer or a Reddit comment. That's the distribution advantage.

Move 4 · Package

Turn the position into SKUs

A company with no treatment to sell monetises by helping you get certain and track whether things improved. The bundles in section 05 are the position made physical.

The receipts (all in production today)
  • LiveLow T routes to a GP referral with zero upsell, enforced in code
  • LivePrintable GP handoff summary with marker table and questions to ask
  • Live"We do not sell your data": CSV export + erasure requests, currently buried in /account
  • LiveNamed GMC-registered GP (verifiable, GMC 4758565) behind the report logic
  • Live"What this test did not tell you" honesty note on normal results
  • LiveUKAS ISO 15189-accredited lab, stated without naming a middleman brand

Domination is four compounding loops

Become the default neutral answer. Every GEO citation, comparison-hub listing and Reddit recommendation compounds toward one goal state: when a UK man asks where to test without getting funnelled, the internet answers with our name. First mover on a claim owns the claim; every later entrant becomes "the other conflict-free one."

Turn trust into data nobody else has. Quiz + post-purchase surveys give first-party evidence on what the mainstream £99 buyer wants, the input the whole market is missing. We steer on data while competitors guess.

Turn retests into a relationship. The retest loop converts one-off buyers into a longitudinal relationship, which is where we eventually earn the right to a tracking product from customers who trust us, instead of fighting Thriva for it cold.

Own the supply chain at volume. Brand volume converts into margin and removes the reseller vulnerability: the already-decided disintermediation path, re-approached from strength.

Why the bundle has to manufacture retention rather than exploit it: the category's buyer is episodic (34% of UK private-health users last year were first-timers; only 11% habitually go private), and no incumbent has proven repeat testing exists at scale: the one category retention claim found in the research died 0-3 in verification. There is no retest habit to capture. Prepaying the second kit is how one gets created, and if it works, the retest-completion rate becomes evidence no competitor has.

The paragraph a treatment funnel cannot write

"If your first result is normal, you don't need the second test. We'll bank it as your 6–12 month recheck, or give you the money back." Default: bank it (matches the live retest cadence and reminder). Option: refund the unused kit, offered plainly. A funnel would be refunding its own conversion event. This kills the "wasteful purchase" objection and is the best proof point on the page.

The research handed us the precedent and the anti-pattern in one finding. Numan's "fear nothing" test (£128, refund if all normal) proves the reassurance-refund mechanic sells. The ASA then ruled its ad misleading: only 4–10% of users qualified for the refund, and the attached subscription was buried. Our version is the documented inverse: refund stated plainly, qualification obvious, nothing recurring hidden underneath.

Before building: (1) confirm with the lab that order time and dispatch time can separate, and get kit shelf life in writing; (2) one terms paragraph for the solicitor: banked-kit validity (12 months is natural), address changes, cancellation rights. Ewa signs the confirmation interval — and her pack gains a defensibility line from the research: the BMJ criticised Numan's quarterly retest cadence against RCPath minimum intervals, while our shapes (one confirmation retest, a 90-day nutrient retest, then 6–12 months) sit inside defensible practice. State it as a feature.

06What it means for supplements

The position disciplines them, then powers them.

The obvious attack: "you don't sell testosterone, you just sell supplements off the back of the test." Three rules defuse it, and two are already built. The plan itself survives intact: same ~£5,950 capped bet, same attach-and-tenure economics, same day 15–45 churn focus.

1

The offer is result-independent. Sacred.

Every kit buyer sees the same supplement offer regardless of result (locked Option 4), including the all-clear maintenance offer. An upsell funnel shows the offer when you're deficient; we show it either way, so a bad result earns us nothing extra. This is the supplement version of "no reason to find a problem." And the research sized the audience this rule serves: Medichecks refers only ~7% of customers to a GP, so roughly 93% of the category finishes with no escalation. The all-clear majority is the market.

2

Supplements answer nutrient results, never hormone results.

Low vitamin D and an EFSA-approved vitamin D supplement is an honest pairing. Low testosterone and a supplement is the dishonest pairing the market runs on. The classifier already enforces it: low T goes to a GP with no upsell. Daily Stack attaches to Kit 2 territory, never to the Kit 1 result card. No "booster" language, ever.

3

The Prove-It bundle is the supplement's engine.

Nobody else can say "take it for 90 days, then we'll retest you and you'll see the numbers." The generalists haven't built the supplement line; the supplement industry would never invite the measurement. The retention product and the trust story become the same object.

07What it does to the financial model

The model stops hoping and starts manufacturing its own inputs.

1

The two make-or-break unknowns become designed outcomes

Attach: the Prove-It bundle creates a second offer moment (the day-90 result) in front of the highest-intent audience possible: someone who already paid to measure change. Tenure: the day-90 retest lands right after the day 15–45 churn window, and "your numbers moved" is the strongest renewal event a supplement subscription can have. The bundles are machines built to move exactly the two variables the model says decide profitability.

2

Cash timing flips in our favour

The customer pays for two kits on day 0; the second kit's cost lands around day 80. Every bundle sale pulls roughly a kit's worth of cash three months forward, which helps Gate 0C (Month-12 cash). The honest offsetting lines: a deferred-revenue liability for undispatched kits, a small on-request refund allowance on the Confirmation bundle's not-low path (which otherwise banks to the recheck), and a banked-kit reserve. Small at current volume, but they go in the model.

3

Kit 1's worst customer becomes the future best customer

Today the low-T buyer is the economic dead end: £38 one-shot, no supplement offer (hormone rule), GP referral, goodbye. The Confirmation bundle raises his AOV now, and post-CQC he is the clinic's launch pipeline (£60–100/month, measured in years). The Phase 0 model still counts this at zero, correctly, but the count of confirmed-low, handoff-in-hand customers becomes a KPI worth tracking today: it is the clinic's day-one revenue base accumulating in advance.

4

What survives, and the one gate wording change

Owned acquisition is still the engine (~£0 CAC; what the positioning and GEO work feed). Cold paid still doesn't scale: even a ~£150 bundle-buyer LTV loses against a ~£200 cold CAC, so the £250 test stays a read, not a channel. Affiliate at ~£30 CAC stays the only profitable paid channel, slightly improved by bundle AOV. Gate 0B stage 1 becomes "CPA below the contribution of the SKU actually sold": £38/53/77 single kits, £48/68/92 by bundle.

Every input now has a measurement date, except one

Bundle take rate: measurable within weeks of shipping. Confirmation trigger rate: our own results data. Refund-vs-bank rate: first all-clear confirmations. Attach and tenure uplift among retesters: Phase 0b. The exception is willingness to pay for £99–179: the 21 Jul research refuted the only affordability evidence, so the quiz Van Westendorp block is the single planned source of that number. That is why it ships with launch. The v2 model doc is in the repo against these working prices (`01_strategy/ltv-cac-profitability-model-2026-07-21.md`).

08What it means for the clinic

The position is the funnel, said honestly.

The apparent contradiction: "no reason to sell you testosterone" from a brand planning a CQC-registered TRT clinic. Walk through what the clinic actually needs, and the position is the best pre-launch it could have. The plan was always that Phase 0 builds a pre-qualified patient pipeline. Look at what the conflict-free machinery produces.

Phase 0
today
Test → confirm → GP handoff. A man tests, gets a low result, gets it properly confirmed by the Confirmation bundle (two morning samples, which a legitimate diagnosis requires anyway), and receives a GP summary pack. Today, the referral goes to an external GP and we earn nothing from it.
CQC lands
The referral destination changes. We hold a pipeline of properly-diagnosed, documentation-in-hand, high-trust candidates. The referral can now go to our own regulated, GMC-GP-led clinic. The clinic doesn't replace the referral. It becomes it.
The kicker
The man with confirmed low T, choosing between a funnel that offered TRT off a quiz in 48 hours and the brand that tested him twice, told him when he was fine, and never pushed: he picks us. Smaller addressable market than the funnels pretend, but the genuine cases are worth £60–100/month for years, and we become their default.

The single most important sentence in the business

Locked by Keith and Ewa before the homepage rewrite. Write the absolute version and the clinic launch becomes a screenshot-able bait-and-switch.

Detonates on clinic launch
"We will never sell testosterone."

Absolute, and false the day the clinic opens. Reddit screenshots it, and every ounce of banked trust converts into evidence against us. Abstinence is the wrong claim.

True today, true post-CQC
"Testing and treatment are separated. We earn the same whether your result is low or fine, and treatment only ever follows an independent clinical decision."

Separation of incentives, not abstinence. True now (low T goes to a GP), and still true when the referral destination is our own clinic, because the claim was never "no treatment": it was "the test doesn't work for the treatment."

What the position demands of the clinic

1

Structurally different, not just verbally

Diagnosis by confirmed morning samples plus symptoms; a prescribing decision that is clinically made and demonstrably declinable ("we say no when the evidence says no" must be operationally true and eventually evidenced). Consider a paid consultation model so clinic revenue isn't contingent on a prescription being issued: the incentive stays clean even inside the clinic.

2

No sales-pressure mechanics

No countdown offers, no "your results qualify you" conversion events, no funnel dressed as a pathway. The intake is the Confirmation bundle plus the GP handoff: clinical pre-work, already productised.

3

Make the regulator a tailwind

UK scrutiny is heading straight at online TRT clinics, and the 21 Jul research documented it: professional press reporting sub-£20 entry tests feeding ~£2,000+ first-year treatment pathways, an Imperial endocrinologist accusing private clinics of "moving the goalposts" on reference ranges, and an ASA ruling already landed on Numan. A clinic whose intake is two confirmed samples and whose brand is built on declining to over-treat gives CQC, the GMC and the ASA nothing to touch. Competitors' funnels are their liability; our restraint compounds into a regulatory moat.
