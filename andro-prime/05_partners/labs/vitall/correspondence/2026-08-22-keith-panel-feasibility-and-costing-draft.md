## Why this replaces the 20 August draft

That draft asked about two markers. The work of 22 August established that **five** panels
sit between us and the supplement range, not two, and Ben's reply cadence is the binding
constraint: he averages about a week per round trip. Five separate asks is five weeks. One
table is one week.

**The trade-off, stated so it is a choice and not an accident:** a five-panel table is a
bigger ask than an eight-cell one and could slow his reply. It is still the right call,
because labs quote at panel level anyway (our own Schedule 1 prices three kits, not
seventeen markers), and because the alternative is serialising a month of latency.

## Design notes (carried from the 20 August draft; the middleman rule still governs)

- **Ben is a DTC competitor as well as our lab.** Everything strategic is stripped. No
  mention of the supplement range these panels feed, no shop, no retest mechanic, no
  cohort or roadmap narrative, no search-demand evidence, no reason we want any of it.
  He gets marker, sample type, volume, timing, cost. That is all he needs to quote.
- **Every cell is a word, a number or a yes/no.** The ask degrades from "research this"
  to "fill this in".
- **The capillary question is pre-empted per panel**, so a no costs no round trip.
- **Collection-timing is asked explicitly.** Fasted and time-of-day requirements are
  customer-behaviour constraints that change the product, not footnotes. Cheaper to learn
  now than after a kit ships.
- **The call is the fallback, not the ask.** Avoids a diary negotiation stalling a table
  he could fill in ten minutes.
- **Our timeline pressure stays vague.** No dates, no launch framing.
- **Combination pricing is asked once, at the end**, because it applies to all five.
